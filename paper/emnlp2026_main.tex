\begin{abstract}
When a linear probe recovers information about a \emph{future} token from a
language model's hidden state, it is tempting to read this as evidence of
planning. We show that such probe accuracies are \emph{baseline-dependent} and
conflate two sources: positional structure present at initialization, and
computation learned during training. We introduce a per-position
\emph{staircase} baseline that compares a target-position probe against the
strongest earlier position, and a \emph{training-dynamics decomposition} that
splits the gap into a positional \emph{floor} (at random initialization) and a
\emph{learned} component. Applying this diagnostic to 19 models across nine
architecture families and five tasks (116 probing experiments), a single
methodology yields three qualitatively different signatures: a large
planning-consistent learned signal for rhyme (in the setting where prior causal
work found planning), a flat positional artifact for code return-types (collapses
under pooling, unmoved by training), and an absent signal for a controlled
QA task. The pattern is consistent across three model scales and, in
representative layerwise analyses, holds at every probed layer rather than
one lucky layer. A within-model checkpoint analysis shows the rhyme probe gap
and behavioral rhyme generation co-emerge during training (Spearman $\rho=0.75$,
$p=0.03$), though post-emergence the relationship does not track magnitude, and
we do not claim the gap ranks unrelated models. We provide a concrete reporting
checklist for future probing studies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Linear probes are a standard tool for asking what information a language
model's hidden states contain \citep{alain2017,belinkov2022,tenney2019}. A
recent and striking application probes for information about \emph{future}
tokens: if a hidden state at position $t$ encodes a property of a token the
model has not yet generated, this may reflect that the model is
\emph{planning} ahead. Causal steering work supports this interpretation in some
settings: \citet{maar2026} show that steering vectors at intermediate positions
can manipulate future rhymes and question answers in models from 1B parameters
upward.

Consider a concrete case. A model reads a couplet's first line ending in
\emph{``sing''} and is about to generate a second line. A probe at the newline
position (after ``sing'') predicts rhyme class at 97\%, while a probe at
earlier positions---including ``sing'' itself---reaches only $\sim$37\%. Is
the newline position \emph{encoding a planning representation} that will guide
generation, or is this cue localization: the model has now attended to
``sing'' and its representation is more linearly separable than at the word's
own position? The raw probe accuracy cannot distinguish these. More generally,
probing classifiers can exploit confounds and recover information that is not
\emph{used} by the model \citep{hewitt2019,belinkov2022,voita2020}, and
because probing is far cheaper than causal intervention it is tempting to treat
probe accuracy as mechanistic evidence. The central methodological question of
this paper: \emph{when a probe recovers future-token information, how much
reflects model computation versus information already recoverable from earlier
positions?}

We argue this question has three distinct quantities: (i) raw target-position
accuracy; (ii) the \emph{gap} against the strongest earlier position; and (iii)
the \emph{learned} component of that gap, isolated by subtracting the step-0
floor. A signal can have high raw accuracy and a large gap yet a near-zero
learned component---in which case the probe reads positional structure, not
acquired computation. We make three contributions.

\textbf{(1) Per-position staircase baseline.} Comparing the target probe against
the single strongest earlier position is a stricter baseline than prior
mean-pooled approaches; tasks that look like planning under weak baselines often
do not survive this comparison (Section~\ref{sec:staircase}).

\textbf{(2) Training-dynamics decomposition.} The step-0 gap isolates what is
purely positional; the gain over training is what we call the learned component
(Section~\ref{sec:decomp}). This is, to our knowledge, a novel way to
separate positional artifacts from acquired computation in future-token probing.

\textbf{(3) Large-scale evaluation.} 19 models, nine architecture families,
five tasks, 116 probing experiments, full layer curves, two baselines,
function-name controls, logit-lens corroboration, and behavioral validation.

The diagnostic is not a planning verdict but a discriminating tool
(Figure~\ref{fig:regimes}): the same methodology yields a growing,
baseline-robust, training-acquired signal for rhyme; a flat, pool-collapsible
artifact for code; and a flat-zero result for QA-neutral. We close with a
checklist for what future probing studies should report.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Probing and its controls.} Linear probing
\citep{alain2017,conneau2018} is widely used but probe accuracy conflates
representation content with probe capacity \citep{hewitt2019}.
Information-theoretic and MDL-based formulations \citep{voita2020,pimentel2020}
make the same point. Our staircase is in this tradition: a position-matched
difference replaces absolute accuracy, and the step-0 floor plays the role of a
control task derived from the model itself.

\paragraph{Future-token information.} Future Lens \citep{pal2023} decodes future
tokens from hidden states; \citet{maar2026} provide causal steering evidence for
implicit planning in rhyme and QA; work on emergent world representations probes
state the model later acts on \citep{li2023,gurnee2024}. Our work is
complementary and diagnostic: we ask how much of a probing signal survives a
stricter baseline and how much is acquired during training.

\paragraph{Reading hidden states across layers.} The logit lens
\citep{nostalgebraist2020} and tuned lens \citep{belrose2023} read hidden states
through the unembedding; layerwise probing shows information is distributed
across depth \citep{tenney2019}, and FFN layers act as key-value memories
\citep{geva2021}. We use the logit lens as an independent, classifier-free check
on the code domain (Section~\ref{sec:robust}).

\paragraph{Training dynamics.} Capabilities emerge at identifiable training
steps: induction heads \citep{olsson2022}, grokking \citep{nanda2023}, knowledge
acquisition \citep{mcgrath2022}. The Pythia suite \citep{biderman2023} provides
intermediate checkpoints. We use checkpoints to define a positional floor and to
test behavioral co-emergence.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The per-position staircase}
\label{sec:staircase}

Each task provides prompts $x$ with a categorical label $y$ for a future token.
For a model with hidden state $h^{(\ell)}_p(x)$ at layer $\ell$ and position $p$,
and a designated target position $t(x)$, we train a probe at the target and at
every earlier position $p < t$. Target positions are defined by interpretable
\emph{resolvers} (e.g.\ the newline after the first couplet line; the last word
before the newline); earlier positions are all token positions strictly before the
target in the same prompt.

Writing $\mathrm{acc}^{(\ell)}_p$ for the cross-validated accuracy of a probe at
layer $\ell$, position $p$, the \emph{staircase gap} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gap}
\Delta^{(\ell)} = \mathrm{acc}^{(\ell)}_t - \max_{p < t}\, \mathrm{acc}^{(\ell)}_p.
\end{equation}
The baseline is the single strongest earlier position. A positive gap is
necessary (not sufficient) evidence that the target adds future-token
information beyond what is already linearly recoverable earlier.

\paragraph{Layer selection and non-max summaries.} We report the headline gap as
the largest-magnitude $\Delta^{(\ell)}$ across probed layers and resolvers, with
a 500-sample bootstrap confidence interval. Because this is a maximum over layers
and resolvers, it is susceptible to selection inflation. We therefore also report
the mean, median, and final-third-mean gap over all probed layers for all 19
models, and show full per-layer curves in Section~\ref{sec:layers}. The task
ordering is preserved under every summary (Appendix~\ref{app:nonmax}).

\subsection{Dual baselines}
\label{sec:dual}

As a second, independent baseline we probe the \emph{mean-pooled} activations
over all earlier positions (the $N{+}P$ baseline of prior work), yielding
$\Delta_{\text{mp}}$. A genuine target-position signal should survive both
baselines; a positional artifact may survive a single-position comparison yet
collapse when earlier positions are pooled.

\subsection{Training-dynamics decomposition}
\label{sec:decomp}

Let $\Delta_0$ be the headline gap at random initialization and
$\Delta_{\text{final}}$ the gap after training. We define
\begin{align}
\text{floor} = \Delta_0, \qquad \text{learned} = \Delta_{\text{final}} - \Delta_0.
\end{align}
The floor is attributable to position and embedding; the learned component is
what training adds. We test significance with an example-level permutation test
but emphasize \emph{magnitude}: with hundreds of examples a $+2$pp learned
component can be significant yet behaviorally negligible.

\subsection{Probing setup}
\label{sec:probing}

Probes are logistic-regression classifiers on PCA-reduced activations
(128 components; PCA fit \emph{inside each training fold} to prevent leakage).
Cross-validation is stratified by label; for QA-neutral we use grouped
cross-validation (\texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold}) and cluster bootstrap to prevent
leakage across minimal pairs (Appendix~\ref{app:grouped}). Two-layer MLP probes
are used on a subset of models to test linearity-artifact explanations. All
hyperparameters are fixed before analysis.

\paragraph{Rhyme.} The prompt is the \emph{first line} of a couplet, formatted
as \texttt{"A~rhyming~couplet:\textbackslash n\{line\}"}, where \texttt{\{line\}}
ends with the rhyme-cue word followed by \texttt{\textbackslash n} (e.g.\ ``The
morning light brings birds that sweetly sing\textbackslash n''). The label is the
rhyme family of the upcoming (not-yet-generated) second-line word; all lines in
one family share the same ending sound. The target resolvers are \texttt{newline}
(the \texttt{\textbackslash n} after the cue word) and \texttt{last\_word\_before\_newline}
(the cue word itself). The earlier baseline includes all positions before the
target, including the cue word. The key finding: the newline position encodes the
rhyme family with $\sim$97\% accuracy, while even the cue word itself achieves
only $\sim$37\%---showing the representation becomes more linearly separable at
the boundary than at the word's own position. This is not novel future-token
prediction (the rhyme constraint is established by the cue word); rather, it
measures how clearly the constraint is \emph{encoded} at the planning-relevant
position \citep{maar2026}. A test using line-2 positions (before the rhyme word
has appeared) is in Appendix~\ref{app:line2} and is important future validation.

\paragraph{Code.} The prompt is a Python function signature. The label is the
return type (5 classes: \texttt{int}, \texttt{str}, \texttt{float}, \texttt{bool},
\texttt{list}); the target is the position before the return annotation. The
signature carries return-type cues, so earlier positions should match the target.

\paragraph{QA-neutral.} Questions paired with minimal counterparts sharing
\emph{identical} surface text across answer classes. Labels are not recoverable
from surface tokens; minimal-pair members are kept together in cross-validation.

\paragraph{QA-suggestive / Trivia.} Weak and null controls respectively.
Trivia saturates bag-of-words; QA-suggestive has limited model coverage and is
not a primary result.

\label{sec:results}

Figure~\ref{fig:regimes} shows the central result: decomposing each task's gap
into a positional floor and a learned component separates the three main tasks
into distinct regimes. The rest of this section validates each axis.

\subsection{The gap varies by an order of magnitude across tasks}
\label{sec:res-cross}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig2_cross_model.pdf}
\caption{Per-position staircase gap by task (one point per model, colored by
family; bars are task means). Rhyme ($+64$pp mean) separates from code and
QA-suggestive ($\sim+12$pp), QA-neutral ($-1.2$pp), and trivia ($0$). The
ordering holds across all nine families.}
\label{fig:cross}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:cross} shows the headline gap for every (model, task) pair.
Rhyme yields a large positive gap on all 19 models (mean $+63.6$pp, range
$[+48.5, +76.5]$); code and QA-suggestive yield small positive gaps ($+12.0$
and $+12.7$pp mean); QA-neutral yields a small negative gap (mean $-1.2$pp);
and trivia yields exactly zero, saturated by bag-of-words. Paired Wilcoxon tests
confirm rhyme exceeds code (median $+53$pp, $n{=}18$, $p<10^{-4}$) and
QA-neutral (median $+72$pp, $n{=}12$, $p<10^{-3}$). We treat these tests as
descriptive: models are not independent (shared architectures, data, tokenizers),
so $p$-values indicate effect magnitude, not frequentist guarantees.

Raw code accuracy exceeds 90\% on most models---but the gap is only $\sim$12pp,
and most of that is positional. A positive probe accuracy is not a positive gap.

\subsection{The gap is broad across layers, not one lucky layer}
\label{sec:layers}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig7_layer_curves.pdf}
\caption{Per-layer target accuracy (solid) vs.\ strongest-earlier baseline
(dashed) for Gemma-2-2B. The rhyme gap spans \emph{every} layer; code is

\label{fig:layers}

Figure~\ref{fig:layers} shows the full layer curves for Gemma-2-2B. For rhyme
the gap is large and consistent at every layer (target $\approx$80--100\% vs.\

rhyme (19 models), $+5.4$ to $+11.0$pp for code (18 models), and exactly
$+0.0$pp for QA-neutral (12 models, to two decimal places). See
Appendix~\ref{app:nonmax} for the full table. The task-ordering---and the
regime assignment---is invariant to layer-summary choice.

\subsection{Training dynamics decompose the gap}
\label{sec:dynamics}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig1_training_dynamics.pdf}
\caption{Staircase gap across training (Pythia-1.4B, log-scale steps). Rhyme
starts at $+34$pp (positional floor) and \emph{grows} to $+58$pp; code is
\emph{flat} near $+11$pp; QA-neutral sits at zero throughout.}
\label{fig:dynamics}
\end{figure}

Probing across checkpoints reveals \emph{why} the tasks differ
(Figure~\ref{fig:dynamics}). At step~0, rhyme shows a $+34$pp floor (rhyme
labels partially correlate with word-identity even in embeddings); the gap grows
to $+58$pp over training, adding $+23.5$pp of learned signal. Code starts at a
$+10$pp floor and stays flat (learned $+2$pp): training adds essentially
nothing at the target position. QA-neutral sits at zero at every checkpoint
including step~0.

A permutation test finds the learned component significant for both rhyme
($p<10^{-3}$) and code ($p{=}9{\times}10^{-4}$) on Pythia-1.4B---but
\emph{magnitudes} tell the story: $+2$--$4$pp learned for code versus
$+19$--$26$pp for rhyme. The decomposition is consistent across all three
Pythia scales (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}, Figure~\ref{fig:decomp}).

\subsection{Mean-pool baseline confirms code is positional}
\label{sec:res-dual}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\columnwidth]{fig4_dual_baseline.pdf}
\caption{Max-earlier ($x$) vs.\ mean-pool ($y$) baseline gap. Rhyme persists

Under the mean-pool baseline (Figure~\ref{fig:dual}), the code gap collapses to

\subsection{Behavioral validation: co-emergence, not magnitude tracking}
\label{sec:behav}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig5_behavioral.pdf}
\caption{Within-model behavioral validation (Pythia-1.4B). The rhyme staircase
gap and behavioral rhyme generation co-emerge between steps 4k--32k. Spearman
$\rho=0.75$ ($p=0.03$), $n=8$.}
\label{fig:behav}
\end{figure}

We track behavioral rhyme-generation accuracy (pronunciation-dictionary scoring;
50 generations per checkpoint) alongside the probe gap across 8 Pythia-1.4B
checkpoints (Figure~\ref{fig:behav}). Both are at floor at step~0 (0\%
generation, $+34$pp probe) and rise together between steps 4k--32k. The
within-model Spearman correlation across all 8 checkpoints is
$\rho=0.75$, $p=0.03$; Pearson $r=0.88$, $p=0.004$.

We are explicit about what this does and does not show. The correlation is
driven by \emph{co-emergence}: both the probe gap and behavioral accuracy go
from floor to above-floor together. \emph{Post}-emergence (steps 4k--143k), the
probe gap saturates while behavioral accuracy fluctuates (32\%--62\%); excluding
step~0, the correlation drops to $\rho=0.63$, $p=0.13$ ($n{=}7$, not
significant). The signal is skill co-emergence, not continuous magnitude
tracking. The 95\% binomial confidence intervals on behavioral accuracy are wide
(e.g.\ step-32k: 31/50, CI [48\%, 76\%]), reflecting that 50 generations per
checkpoint is a thin behavioral estimate. The \emph{cross-model} correlation is
not significant ($\rho=0.17$, $p=0.52$, $n{=}17$): the probe gap does not rank
unrelated models by generation quality, and we do not use it as such.

\subsection{Robustness: probe family, name controls, and logit lens}
\label{sec:robust}

\paragraph{MLP probes.} Two-layer MLP probes agree with linear probes in sign
and regime on 13/13 runs across six models and four families
(Appendix~\ref{app:mlp}).

\paragraph{Code name controls (four models).} If the code signal is carried by
earlier signature positions, (i) removing informative names should not hurt and
(ii) misleading names should hurt. Both hold across four models spanning three
families (Table~\ref{tab:names}). Replacing names with opaque placeholders
(\texttt{func\_000}) leaves accuracy essentially unchanged (mean
$\Delta{=}+0.007$); adversarial names crash accuracy to near chance ($0.31$--$0.39$).
We treat this as a sanity check: the probe reads earlier-position cues, and
misleading cues defeat it.

\paragraph{Logit lens (code).} Reading hidden states through the model's
unembedding at the \emph{final layer} (no classifier selection), return-type
accessibility grows over training: Pythia-2.8B $0.13\rightarrow0.52$; Pythia-410M
$0.21\rightarrow0.52$ (Table~\ref{tab:logit}). This classifier-free result
confirms the model learns to represent return types; read alongside the flat code
staircase, it shows that a growing representation need not be localized to the
target position---exactly the distinction the staircase detects.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the staircase measures---and what it does not.} A positive,
baseline-robust, training-acquired staircase gap (as for rhyme) is the strongest
\emph{observational} probing signal achievable short of intervention, and it is
in the same setting where \citet{maar2026} find causal planning. But the rhyme
task has the cue word in the prompt (Line 1 ends with the rhyme word before the
target newline); the probe is measuring how clearly the rhyme constraint is
encoded at the boundary position, not predicting a novel unseen word. We call
this a \emph{planning-consistent} signal to reflect both the positive evidence
and the interpretive limitation. A line-2 experiment (Appendix~\ref{app:line2})
would provide stronger evidence; we provide a script and treat it as important
future validation.

\paragraph{QA-neutral is not a refutation of Maar et al.} The QA-neutral null
is a stricter, surface-controlled diagnostic: identical surface text removes any
earlier-position cue by construction. A null here does not claim QA planning is
absent in the less controlled settings where steering succeeds; it shows the
staircase does not manufacture a positive gap just because a task involves a
future answer.

\paragraph{Why the negative tasks are the contribution.} The diagnostic's value
is that it discriminates. Rhyme: growing learned signal. Code: flat positional
artifact. QA-neutral: flat zero. A diagnostic returning ``planning'' everywhere
would be useless.

\paragraph{Size invariance.} The rhyme gap is $+48$--$+77$pp from GPT-2 small
(124M) to Gemma-27B, with no monotonic size trend. This is a property of the
task-representation interaction, not a capability score: the signal is already
detectable at small scale, though the causal evidence for its role in generation
is independent of size.

\paragraph{Checklist for future probing.} Report: (1) raw target accuracy;
(2) gap against the \emph{strongest earlier position}; (3) a \emph{mean-pool}
baseline; (4) the step-0 \emph{floor} if checkpoints exist; (5) the
\emph{learned} component; (6) full layer curves or non-max summaries;
(7) grouped splits and leakage checks; (8) behavioral validation
\emph{within-model across training}, not across unrelated models.

\section{Conclusion}

We introduced a per-position staircase baseline and a training-dynamics
decomposition for diagnosing future-token probing claims, applying them across
19 models, nine architecture families, and five tasks. The diagnostic separates
a planning-consistent learned signal (rhyme), a positional artifact (code), and
a genuine null (QA-neutral). Probe accuracy alone does not establish planning;
the appropriate evidence is a positive gap against a strong position baseline,
decomposed against the positional floor.

\section*{Limitations}

Our diagnostic is observational. A positive staircase gap is necessary but not
sufficient evidence for planning; a null gap does not establish absent
computation. The rhyme task has the cue word in the prompt (Line 1); the gap
measures constraint encoding at the planning-relevant position, not prediction
of an unseen word---the causal claim rests on \citet{maar2026}, not on our
probes. The training-dynamics decomposition is demonstrated on Pythia only
(checkpoints unavailable for Gemma, Qwen, Llama). The within-model behavioral
correlation is on one model scale (Pythia-1.4B, 8 checkpoints, 50 generations
each); post-emergence the correlation is not significant ($\rho=0.63$, p=0.13, n=7),
and we do not claim the gap tracks behavioral magnitude continuously. The
QA-suggestive and trivia tasks have thin model coverage and are controls, not
primary results. Logit-lens corroboration is available for the code domain only
(final layer; prior best-layer result had the same selection issue we critique).
We did not run a full PCA-dimension or random-seed sensitivity sweep and report
this as an open item.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work analyzes publicly available pretrained language models on synthetic
and benchmark linguistic tasks. No new models or data are released beyond derived
probing results. The main societal relevance is methodological caution against

\section{Grouped cross-validation for QA-neutral}
\label{app:grouped}

The QA-neutral task pairs prompts with minimal-pair counterparts sharing
identical surface text. Ungrouped cross-validation inflates accuracy by letting
the probe memorize pair identity; \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold} with cluster
bootstrap removes this, moving the BoW baseline to chance and the headline gap
to $-1.2$pp. All QA-neutral numbers use grouped CV.

\section{Rhyme line-2 experiment (future validation)}
\label{app:line2}

The rhyme staircase probes at the end of Line 1 (the newline after the
cue word). A skeptical reviewer asks: would the gap persist at positions
\emph{within Line 2}, before the rhyme word has appeared? If yes, this would
provide stronger planning evidence, since the earlier baseline would then
include the cue word (at Line 1's position) and the Line 2 positions have not
yet seen any rhyme word. A script for this experiment---constructing prompts
with Line 2 prefixes appended and probing at those positions---is provided in
\texttt{scripts/lookahead/experiments/rhyme\_line2\_target.py}. We flag this
as important future validation and intend to add it to the final version. As a
reference: if the Line-2 gap (target in Line 2) over earlier positions
(including the cue word) is comparable to the Line-1 gap reported here, the
planning-consistent interpretation is substantially strengthened.
