\begin{abstract}
As a multi-turn conversation accumulates, instruction-tuned LLMs show two vivid
``context-fatigue'' signatures: output entropy collapses several-fold, and the last
token's attention drifts off the system prompt and the current query onto recent
context. We reproduce both across Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and the full
OLMo-2-7B post-training chain---and then show, with a design that other context-rot
studies cannot use, that they are \emph{not} a competence decay. Our key methodological
choice is to accumulate \textbf{individually localized tasks} (self-contained multiple-choice
cases) rather than one long task, so the information needed for the current answer is
always at the current query and never diluted across length. This \emph{structurally rules
out attention dilution / lost-in-the-middle} as the cause. With dilution removed, the
signatures persist but performance does not move: per-case accuracy is flat-to-rising with
context fill ($r{\approx}0$ even when in-context learning is deterred with a random-subject
stream), checkable instruction adherence stays at ceiling, and---conditioning on fill---the
cases the model gets \emph{wrong} are the ones where it \emph{fixates} on the current query,
while correct answers spread attention onto the accumulated context (the per-case fingerprint
of in-context learning). The entropy collapse vanishes on organic WildChat dialogue ($0.99$
within-conversation late/early ratio) and tracks conversation \emph{homogeneity}, not length.
An OLMo-2 dose-response shows the collapse is a baseline-confidence \emph{level shift}
installed mostly by supervised fine-tuning, and a causal test of the boundary-detector
feature blamed for it does not rescue the model. We argue ``context fatigue'' as measured is
in-context comfort, not decay; the genuine hazard is a widening confidently-wrong gap.
\end{abstract}

\section{The claim under test}

A recurring worry about long conversations is \emph{context rot}: as turns accumulate, the
model is said to degrade. The phenomenology is real and easy to elicit. On a repeating
multiple-choice task, instruction-tuned models become dramatically more confident as context
fills---next-token entropy drops $3$--$4\times$ on Qwen-2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B, in both
question orders---and the last token's attention shifts systematically: away from the system
prompt and the current query, toward the most recent prior cases, with the attention
distribution diffusing. The tempting reading is that the model is being worn down: it stops
reading its instructions, fixates on recent text, and its collapsing entropy is overconfidence
that should translate into errors and into sycophantic capitulation when challenged.

This abstract tests that reading and finds it inverted. The signatures are robust and
replicate across four model families, but they are signatures of \emph{in-context learning},
not of degradation. We make the case with a design that separates accumulation from the
confound that dominates the existing literature.

\section{Design: localized tasks rule out attention dilution}

Most context-rot evaluations embed the answer-bearing information inside \emph{one long
task}---a long document, a buried ``needle,'' a growing codebase. There, longer context
mechanically \emph{dilutes} the relevant tokens among irrelevant ones, so any degradation
conflates two things: the cost of accumulation per se and the cost of the target being
harder to attend to (``lost in the middle,'' \citealp{liu2023lost}). These cannot be told
apart in that setup.

Our harness instead accumulates \textbf{individually localized tasks}: a stream of
self-contained $5$-option medical cases (DDXPlus, \citealp{tchango2022ddxplus}) or discrete
factual questions (MMLU, \citealp{hendrycks2021mmlu}). Each answer depends only on the
\emph{current} question, which always sits at the end of the context; prior turns are never
required to answer it. Accumulation therefore grows the context without ever diluting the
information the current answer needs. \textbf{This structurally removes attention dilution as
a candidate mechanism}---if performance is flat here, it cannot be rescued or explained by
dilution---and lets us read accumulation effects cleanly. A reversed-question-order control
additionally removes any difficulty-ordering confound (the entropy collapse is order
invariant). Because a $4$--$32$k window holds only tens of cases, we pool many independent
accumulation sessions (different case and option orders) to power per-case analysis.

\section{The signatures are real---and they replicate}

The attention reallocation is not an artifact of our attention reconstruction: we replicate
OLMo-2's post-RoPE last-token attention and validate it against the model's own
\texttt{output\_attentions} to $\max|\Delta|\approx 1.4\mathrm{e}{-3}$. With context fill it
produces strong, monotone, per-case correlations at every probed layer
(layer 24: system$\leftrightarrow$fill $-0.93$, recent$\leftrightarrow$fill $+0.89$,
current$\leftrightarrow$fill $-0.71$, entropy$\leftrightarrow$fill $+0.95$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:main}c). The DPO and Instruct checkpoints are indistinguishable, so the
reallocation is present from the SFT/DPO stage, not introduced by the final RLVR step. The
entropy collapse likewise replicates across model families. By every visual diagnostic, this
is a real, strongly-measurable effect that grows with context---exactly the thing one would
report as ``context rot.''

\section{...but there is no performance cost}

\paragraph{Accuracy is flat, and stays flat when ICL is removed.}
Per-case accuracy does not fall as context fills; it is worst at the cold start and warms up
($\mathrm{corr}(\text{correct},\text{fill})=+0.11$ Instruct, $+0.07$ DPO). The obvious
objection is that a homogeneous repeating task lets in-context learning mask a hidden decay.
We remove that crutch: in a \emph{random-subject} stream each question is drawn uniformly from
all 56 MMLU subjects, so prior context carries no predictive information about the next
question---only the answer \emph{format} is learnable. Accuracy is still flat across the first
$\sim$80\% of context ($0.57\to0.69$, $\mathrm{corr}=-0.00$), versus a coherent single-subject
stream ($0.84$, $\mathrm{corr}=-0.01$); the lower level just reflects harder questions, not a
slope (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}b). With dilution already excluded by design, neither accumulation
nor the loss of ICL produces decay.

\paragraph{Instruction adherence holds.}
Looking for a competence-preserved, compliance-degraded dissociation, we add a checkable
``canary'' system instruction (begin every reply with a marker; end with a tag) and track
obedience versus fill across baseline, forced-history, and refresh arms. Adherence is perfect
throughout ($\mathrm{corr}(\text{violation},\text{fill})=0$) with accuracy flat---an honest
null: the canaries were easy enough to sit on a ceiling, so this bounds rather than proves the
effect, but it shows no adherence decay where the degradation reading predicts one.

\paragraph{The per-case mechanism inverts the story.}
Conditioning on context fill, the cases OLMo-2 answers \emph{wrong} have \emph{higher}
current-query attention and lower, more peaked attention entropy than the ones it gets right
(Table~\ref{tab:invert}). Errors are associated with \emph{fixating} on the query; correct
answers spread attention onto the accumulated cases---i.e.\ onto the in-context examples. The
``rot'' (system erosion, recency bias, diffusion) and the within-context predictor of a correct
answer point in \emph{opposite} directions. More contextual attention is the fingerprint of
the ICL that keeps accuracy up, not a symptom of decay.

\section{The entropy collapse is in-context comfort, not depth}

If the collapse were a generic depth effect it should survive on heterogeneous dialogue. It
does not. On 150 organic WildChat conversations \citep{zhao2024wildchat} (Qwen-2.5-7B,
teacher-forced over the real history), within-conversation entropy is flat with depth: median
late/early ratio $0.99$, within-conversation $\mathrm{corr}(\text{entropy},\text{depth})$
median $-0.02$, and 52\% of conversations trend down---a coin flip (Fig.~\ref{fig:main}d). The
$3$--$4\times$ DDXPlus collapse simply does not appear once the task stops repeating.
Partitioning WildChat by its \emph{own} inter-turn homogeneity (format and dataset held
constant) isolates the driver: the entropy slope correlates with homogeneity
(partial $r=-0.151$ controlling for length), homogeneous conversations collapse
($0.897$ late/early) while heterogeneous ones are flat ($1.001$), and because homogeneous
conversations are \emph{longer}, length works against the effect. The collapse is the model
growing comfortable with a repeating task---in-context learning sharpening a distribution---not
fatigue with depth.

\section{It is an instruction-tuning level shift, and the proposed mechanism does not hold}

Replaying one fatigue probe across OLMo-2-7B's \texttt{base}$\to$\texttt{SFT}$\to$\texttt{DPO}
$\to$\texttt{Instruct} chain (identical cases and orders, so differences are attributable only
to post-training) turns a single correlational base-vs-IT pair into a graded curve.
Early-context entropy collapses monotonically, $1.06\to0.58\to0.33\to0.20$ ($\sim$5$\times$),
with the \emph{largest single drop at base$\to$SFT}: supervised fine-tuning on assistant
transcripts installs most of the overconfidence, before any preference optimization
(Fig.~\ref{fig:main}a). But the ``collapses \emph{as context fills}'' framing is mostly a
base-model ICL effect plus a floor: the within-context ratio \emph{falls} across stages
($1.64\to0.47$), because aligned models start near a confidence floor and have nowhere left to
drop. Within a fixed window, post-training is a downward \emph{level shift} in baseline
entropy, not a steeper collapse slope.

Finally, the mechanistic story does not survive a causal test. A boundary-detector SAE feature
(Gemma Scope F90871, \citealp{lieberum2024gemmascope}) had been singled out as the regulator
suppressed under fatigue. Clamping it back to its clean level on held-out fatigued cases moves
entropy the \emph{wrong} way ($0.442\to0.465$ vs.\ clean $0.293$), leaves accuracy flat, and
barely moves sycophancy; a random feature of similar magnitude perturbs entropy at least as
much, and the feature's decision-token suppression is sign-unstable across configurations. The
single-feature mechanism is a context-dependent correlate, not a lever.

\section{What context fatigue actually is}

Across four model families and an organic-dialogue control, with attention dilution removed by
construction: accumulation produces a real, monotone reallocation of attention and a real
collapse of \emph{confidence}, but no loss of accuracy, no loss of instruction adherence, and a
within-context error signature that is the opposite of neglect. The confidence collapse is
in-context comfort with a homogeneous task, installed by instruction tuning as a baseline
level shift. ``Context fatigue,'' as the signatures measure it, is in-context learning, not
decay. The genuine hazard is therefore not falling competence but \emph{calibration}: as the
model grows confident at a flat accuracy, the confidently-wrong gap widens---most consequential
exactly where a homogeneous high-stakes stream makes the model surest.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Per-condition $n$ is bounded by the context window (tens of cases per session; we pool
sessions); the OLMo sycophancy denominators are small and inconclusive; the instruction-canary
null is a floor effect; and the WildChat figures are summary values from teacher-forced
analyses. The accuracy claim is strongest for localized tasks---genuinely length-dependent
single-task settings (where dilution is the point) are out of scope by design.
